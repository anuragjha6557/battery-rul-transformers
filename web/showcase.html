How long will this battery last? A leakage-free benchmark with calibrated uncertainty

An end-to-end rebuild of an IEEE ITEC 2024 paper on iTransformer-based battery remaining-useful-life prediction. It covers three public datasets, 18 model configurations under one honest protocol, conformal prediction intervals and a live demo.

The question. A lithium-ion cell loses capacity every charge–discharge cycle. Operators want three answers:

  • State of health now: SOH estimation, i.e. capacity as a % of new.
  • SOH over the next 1–200 cycles: forecasting.
  • Cycles until SOH hits 80 %: remaining useful life (RUL).

The source paper reported large gains for iTransformer on 4 NASA and 4 CALCE cells. Those numbers were treated as untrusted. This project rebuilds the evaluation from scratch, so the answer to "does inverted attention help?" can be defended.

Headline. iTransformer has the best mean rank across 40 dataset × task × metric conditions and leads in-distribution on Toyota (124 cells). On the tiny NASA and CALCE sets, a tuned LightGBM is as good or better. The page shows each claim with its evidence, including what didn't work.

1 · Data and exploratory analysis

Three public datasets, kept separate throughout. The cells use different chemistries, form factors and test protocols, which is what makes cross-dataset generalisation hard.

Life is predictable early (Severson 2019)

The log-variance of ΔQ(V) between cycles 100 and 10 against cycle life on Toyota train cells. This is the strongest single early-life signal, and our feature reproduction matches the paper's.

Data quirks that change the answer

NASA: capacity "regenerates" after long rests (marked). Cells B0005–B0007 share one rig and the same rest schedule, so their jumps line up. B0018 is the only independent NASA test cell.

CALCE: about 3 % of cycles lose 13–16 % of capacity because the charger's constant-voltage step ended after one sample (marked). This is an equipment artifact, not ageing, and it sets a 1–2 % SOH noise floor for any forecaster.

End-of-life labelling: a hidden degree of freedom

On CALCE, the first raw crossing of 80 % SOH happens on an artifact dip, hundreds of cycles before the cell truly ages out. This benchmark labels EOL as the first sustained crossing (5 consecutive cycles, artifacts masked). That one choice moves CALCE RUL labels by 200–500 cycles; the paper never states its rule.

Which per-cycle signals track health?

Spearman correlation of each per-cycle charge feature with SOH, by dataset. Charge-phase features are allowed as inputs because they are measured before the discharge that defines capacity (leakage rule: discharge quantities of cycle k are the target itself).

2 · Method: an honest protocol first, models second

Three tasks, never mixed

At a prediction origin (cycle Tp) the model sees only cycles ≤ Tp (the shaded look-back window): A estimates today's SOH from today's charge; B forecasts SOH directly at h = 1…100 (200 on Toyota) cycles ahead; C predicts the cycles until 80 % SOH.

  • Splits. NASA and CALCE use leave-one-cell-out, with inner cross-fitting for early stopping and calibration. Toyota uses the official 41 / 43 / 40 train / test 1 / test 2 split, so the numbers are comparable with the literature.
  • No leakage. Scalers are fitted on training cells only; no future cycles are used; no absolute cycle index is an input. Seven leakage rules are enforced by unit tests, which were checked by injecting leaks and confirming the tests catch them.
  • Fair tuning. Every learned model got the same Optuna budget: 50 trials on Toyota training cells with Hyperband pruning. Hyperparameters then transfer unchanged to NASA and CALCE (no winner's curse on 4 cells). 5 seeds per configuration.
  • Statistics. 95 % cluster-bootstrap CIs over test cells, Holm-corrected Wilcoxon tests on Toyota, and "k / 4 cells" direction consistency on NASA and CALCE. Every number traces to an MLflow run.
  • Models.
    • Naive: persistence, last slope.
    • Classical: linear / double-exponential / power-law fits, Gaussian process.
    • Tabular: ElasticNet, LightGBM.
    • Linear deep: N/D/RLinear.
    • Recurrent: LSTM, GRU.
    • Attention: Transformer, PatchTST, iTransformer in single-channel (SC) and multi-channel (MC) variants.

4 · Uncertainty you can trust

A point forecast is not enough for maintenance planning. Each quantile model predicts the 5–95 % range, then conformalized quantile regression (CQR) widens or narrows that range using errors measured on held-out training cells. The interval then covers the truth about 90 % of the time, as long as the new cell resembles the training cells.

Coverage of the nominal 90 % interval at h = 50: raw model quantiles (hollow) vs after CQR (filled). Target band 85–95 % shaded. CQR fixes severe under-coverage (NASA iTransformer: 46 % → 90 %). It cannot fix distribution shift: on Toyota test 2, GRU, LSTM and LightGBM stay under target, and the demo's out-of-distribution check exists for that reason.

The price of coverage: mean interval width (% SOH) before and after calibration.

5 · What didn't work (pre-registered, reported anyway)

6 · Capacity regeneration and data artifacts

A key motivation of the source paper was capacity regeneration: a cell that rests recovers some capacity, and the next cycles look "younger". This section tests that claim directly, and separates it from a look-alike artifact.

  • NASA: genuine regeneration. Each rest longer than 6 hours is followed by a +3.1 % SOH jump on average (20 events).
  • CALCE: mostly an artifact. In 109 cycles the charger skipped its constant-voltage step, so the next discharge reads about 10.6 % low; true recovery after rests there is only +0.8 %.
  • Toyota: no long rests, so no regeneration.

Estimating SOH right after a rest: inverted attention is the best model

SOH estimation (task A) error on NASA at regeneration cycles vs ordinary cycles, 5 seeds. Here the rest has already happened and is an input. iTransformer (SC and MC) is the most accurate model at regeneration cycles (RMSE 1.87 and 2.18 % SOH vs ≥ 3.17 for every other model). This is the defensible version of the paper's claim.

Forecasting regeneration needs the rest schedule

A forecaster issued before the rest cannot know it is coming, so every benchmark model misses the jump (−3.3 % SOH, the same as persistence). A pre-registered experiment (ADR-0016) gave models the planned rest schedule of the forecast window as known-future inputs. Operators usually know when a cell will sit idle, and in the NASA test the rests are part of the protocol.

Held-out NASA B0018 forecasts issued just before a long rest (▲). The LSTM without the schedule projects the smooth trend. With the planned rests it anticipates the regeneration jump one cycle ahead and decays back afterwards. Forecasts exist at h = 1, 10, 25, 50 and 100.

Error at NASA regeneration cycles without (faded) and with (solid) the planned rest schedule, 5 seeds × 4 held-out cells. The schedule cuts the error by up to 42 % (time-step Transformer) and 26 % for the LSTM, which then captures about 70 % of each jump. Inverted attention gains the least (iTransformer 10 %, SC 4 %): it absorbs these inputs as one extra token, while LSTM, Transformer and LightGBM feed them straight to the output head. Both pre-registered hypotheses (≥ 50 % for iTransformer; attention gains most) are rejected and reported.

CALCE: treating charger glitches as invalid measurements

Paired change from dropping the 109 glitch cycles before training (ADR-0017), with both arms scored on the same glitch-free targets (95 % CIs; filled circle = better, square = worse, open diamond = inconclusive). Forecasts improve for every learned model (e.g. iTransformer-SC 2.43 → 2.14 % SOH at h = 50), but RUL gets slightly worse. Dips near the 80 % line carry information about how close a cell is to end of life. The benchmark therefore keeps raw data and additionally reports CALCE forecasts on glitch-free targets. The demo marks both event types.

7 · The field setting: no capacity history

The lab setting above assumes a measured capacity log. A real battery management system (BMS) has no such log: capacity is the label, never an input. The field setting (ADR-0018, ADR-0020) feeds models only what a BMS can measure during a charge, plus the cycle count. Three tasks: F-A SOH estimation, F-B forecasting, F-C RUL. NASA and CALCE use leave-one-cell-out (4 cells each); Toyota uses the official test 1 / test 2 split. 5 seeds.

Is iTransformer justified here? Verdict: negative

Negative under the pre-registered rule (ADR-0021). Mean rank over the 12 task × condition cells (lower is better): . iTransformer does not have the best rank, and competitors beat it on 2 of 3 tasks on Toyota test 1. Borderline: that second condition depends on what "significantly" means. Under the CI rule or the Holm-Wilcoxon rule alone, competitors win 2 of 3 tasks; only if both must hold for the same competitor does the count drop to 1 and the verdict becomes mixed. We report negative because it holds under each criterion the project has used. Shift-robustness qualifier: on Toyota test 2, a batch with a different rest, CV time and temperature, iTransformer is the most accurate model for F-A and F-B, significantly better than every competitor. Its intervals do not follow: h = 50 coverage there is 0.66 against 0.87 for LightGBM.

The four claims of the iTransformer paper, tested:

1 · Variate tokens exploit many correlated variates: chemistry-conditional

Paired change in SOH-estimation RMSE from adding 15 Q(V) curve points as variates (Stage 1), 95 % cluster-bootstrap CIs. Filled = CI excludes zero. iTransformer gains the most on CALCE and loses on Toyota (LFP plateau).

2 · Long lookbacks help: not supported

Paired change from lookback 32 to 128 (Stage 2). No monotone gain; L = 128 is worse on CALCE forecasting. (512 was dropped by owner decision.)

3 · Known-future covariates as a variate token: not supported

Error change at regeneration targets, 1 cycle ahead, when the planned rest schedule is given as an extra input. Hatched = delivered as an iTransformer variate token; plain = as tabular inputs. Negative = worse than the model without the schedule.

4 · Channel mixing beats channel independence: supported

iTransformer minus PatchTST, same cells and seeds. iTransformer's error is lower in all 12 task × condition cells (CI excludes zero on every Toyota split; wide on 4-cell NASA and CALCE). On CALCE, PatchTST is worse than the age-only baseline.

Intervals: informative on Toyota, not on NASA or CALCE

Per-horizon CQR 90 % intervals at h = 50, mean over seeds. An interval is useful when its width is under 50 % of the predicted distance to 80 % SOH (ADR-0019). On NASA and CALCE almost no origin is useful (the intervals cover, but say little), NASA coverage is also below target for some models, and on Toyota test 2 the batch shift pulls coverage well below 0.90 for most models (shaded band = 0.85–0.95).

RUL intervals (CQR, all origins). Widths of hundreds of cycles on NASA and CALCE exceed the cells' remaining life scale; the field RUL intervals are not decision-grade there.
